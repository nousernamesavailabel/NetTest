What is Bufferbloat?
Bufferbloat happens when a router or modem queues far more data than it needs to before dropping anything. Under a quiet link your ping is low and steady — but the moment a big download or upload saturates that link, its oversized buffers fill up, and every other packet (a video call, a game, a voice call) has to wait in line behind them. Ping can jump from a few milliseconds to hundreds of milliseconds for as long as the link stays busy, even though your "speed" looks fine.
This test measures it directly: it records your idle ping before the download/upload starts, then keeps pinging in the background while the link is fully loaded. The Bufferbloat score above is the worst-case increase between the two, graded like this:
A+< 5 msImperceptible — rock solid for gaming and video calls even at full load.
A< 30 msGreat — very unlikely you'd ever notice added lag.
B< 60 msGood — minor lag possible in the most latency-sensitive apps.
C< 200 msFair — noticeable lag in games or calls while the link is busy.
D< 400 msPoor — video calls stutter and games get hard to play under load.
F≥ 400 msBad — the connection feels like it stalls whenever it's busy.
